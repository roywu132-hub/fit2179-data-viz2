Why National Parks Matter

Australia's national parks protect some of the country's most distinctive landscapes, ecosystems, and cultural sites. They play a crucial role in conserving biodiversity, supporting recreation, and shaping how Australians connect with nature. As these parks vary widely in size, environment, and accessibility, understanding their distribution and characteristics is essential for appreciating their national significance.

This visualisation aims to help Australians explore where national parks are located, how they differ, and why they matter for conservation and public wellbeing.

01 / Geographic Distribution

Where Are Australia's Major National Parks?

The map below plots 14 major national parks across Australia. Circle size represents annual visitor numbers, and colour represents the state or territory. Hover over each circle to see park details including area, establishment year, and visitor count.

Key insight: The most-visited parks (Blue Mountains, Royal NP) are within 2 hours of Sydney. Remote parks like Kakadu — despite their immense size — attract far fewer visitors, highlighting the role of accessibility in park popularity.
02 / Visitors by State

International Visitors by State (2024)

New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland dominate international visitation, reflecting their world-famous parks and proximity to major airports. The Northern Territory — home to iconic Uluru and Kakadu — draws substantially fewer international visitors than its cultural significance might suggest.

Key insight: NSW receives 1.28 million international park visitors annually — over 7× more than the NT. Infrastructure and accessibility, rather than ecological uniqueness, are the primary drivers of visitation.
03 / Growth Trend

Visitor Growth Trend (2015–2024)

National park visitation has grown substantially over the past decade — interrupted only by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. The recovery has been remarkable, with 2024 numbers surpassing pre-pandemic peaks by over 20%. Hover over any data point to see the exact figures.

Key insight: COVID-19 caused a 46% drop in visitors in 2020. However, the subsequent "nature rebound" drove rapid recovery and record-breaking numbers by 2024.
04 & 05 / Top Parks & Size Comparison

Which Parks Draw the Crowds?

Blue Mountains alone accounts for nearly 39% of all visits to the top parks. The donut chart shows market share; toggle to the scatter plot to explore whether bigger parks are actually more popular.

X-axis uses a log scale to show the wide range of park sizes. Hover for details.

Key insight: Royal NP (150 sq km) receives over 17× more visitors per sq km than Kakadu (19,804 sq km). Location beats size every time.
06 / Park History

A Century of Conservation — When Parks Were Created

Australia has been establishing national parks for over 145 years. The dot plot shows each major park by its year of establishment, organised by state. Circle size represents current annual visitor numbers.

Key insight: Royal NP (NSW, 1879) is the second-oldest national park in the world. Many of Australia's most visited parks were established before 1960.
07 / Seasonality

When Do Visitors Come? Seasonal Patterns by Park Type

Australia's national parks span an extraordinary range of climates. Coastal parks peak in summer, mountain parks surge in winter and autumn, and outback parks are only comfortably visited in cooler months. The heatmap reveals these contrasting seasonal patterns.

Key insight: Outback parks receive 4× more visitors in July than January — the opposite of coastal parks. Understanding seasonality is critical for park management and avoiding overcrowding.
08 / Ecosystems

What Types of Ecosystems Are Being Protected?

Australia protects an extraordinary diversity of ecosystems — from tropical rainforests to vast deserts. The chart below shows the proportional ecosystem composition of each state's protected areas. Hover over segments to see exact areas.

Key insight: Queensland protects a high proportion of rainforest — among the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth — including the ancient Daintree Rainforest.
09 / Economic Impact

Tourism Revenue Generated by National Parks (2024)

In 2024, park tourism generated over $8.5 billion AUD in combined domestic and international revenue. NSW and Victoria lead in absolute terms, reflecting their higher visitor volumes and proximity to urban centres.

Key insight: For every dollar invested in national park management, parks generate approximately $4–6 in tourism revenue. Conservation and economic benefit are complementary goals.
10 / UNESCO Status

UNESCO World Heritage — Does It Boost Visitor Numbers?

Australia has 12 UNESCO World Heritage Areas. UNESCO listing signals international significance, but does it translate to higher visitor numbers? Use the buttons below to filter the chart.

Key insight: Blue Mountains (UNESCO listed) tops the chart, but Royal NP (not listed) ranks 2nd. UNESCO status raises international profile, but domestic visitation depends more on proximity to cities.
Conclusion

Protecting Nature, Connecting People

Australia's national parks are among the country's most valuable natural assets. They not only provide unique experiences for locals and international visitors but also play a critical role in biodiversity conservation and cultural preservation.

📍 Location matters more than size. Urban-adjacent parks dominate visitor numbers.
📈 Growth is strong. Post-COVID recovery reached record highs by 2024.
🌏 Diversity of ecosystems — from desert to rainforest — makes Australia's parks globally unique.

Protecting Australia's national parks is not only about conservation — it is also about creating meaningful connections between people and nature, and sustaining the economic and cultural fabric of our communities.
